## 1. Brand overview

**Positioning.** The FP&A Engineer: finance planning built the way engineers build things, tested, traceable and made to be used.

**Mission.** Give leadership teams numbers they can trust and act on the same day.

**Vision.** A finance function that people come to before they decide, not after.

**Values**
- **Rigour.** Every number ties, rolls and traces back to an assumption someone owns.
- **Clarity.** One headline per slide. If a busy executive can't read it in thirty seconds, it isn't finished.
- **Generosity.** Share the method. The cheat-code series exists so other analysts get there faster.

**Personality.** Precise · Warm · Curious · Calm under deadline.

**Audience**
- *Primary:* finance leaders, CFOs and founders hiring or working with FP&A.
- *Secondary:* analysts who follow the cheat-code posts to grow their own craft.

**Why this direction.** Finance brands earn trust with navy, deep green, restrained gold and clean, readable type. That is the base here. The editorial serif and the colourful card wall keep it from feeling like a bank: a person, not an institution.

## 2. Logo

Files: [`site/assets/brand/`](site/assets/brand/)

| Version | File | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Mark | `mark.svg` | Favicon, avatar, slide corner, social profile |
| Wordmark | `wordmark.svg` | Website header, CV, email signature |
| Mark on dark | `mark-reversed.svg` | Navy or dark backgrounds |

**The mark** is a spreadsheet cell: a navy rounded square holding a serif **R**, with a gold cursor in the corner, the selected cell where the work happens.

**The wordmark** is the name in Newsreader with *The FP&A Engineer* beneath it in small caps.

- **Clear space:** the height of the gold cursor on every side.
- **Minimum size:** mark 20 px; wordmark 120 px wide.
- **Don't:** stretch it, recolour the cursor, add shadows or gradients, or set it on a busy photo.

## 3. Colour

The base is quiet: ivory paper and ink. Colour arrives in blocks (cards, bands, charts) so it reads like a well-built dashboard: each colour means something.

| Ink | `#14182B` | 20, 24, 43 | Text, buttons, rules |

### Supporting

|---|---|---|---|

| Peach | `#FFD8C4` | 255, 216, 196 | Testimonial band |

**In charts:** navy for actuals, gold for budget, green for favourable, coral for adverse. Keep that meaning everywhere, including slides.

**Accessibility (measured, WCAG 2.1):** Ink on Ivory 16.6:1, Ink on Peach 13.3:1, Ivory on Ledger Navy 12.7:1, Ivory on Growth Green 9.6:1, Ivory on Variance Coral 5.6:1, Mist on Ivory 4.7:1, Ink on Signal Gold 8.2:1. Signal Gold on Ivory is only 2.0:1. Use Signal Gold for shapes and fills only, never for text on ivory; ink text on a gold fill is fine.

## 4. Typography

| Role | Font | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Display and quotes | **Newsreader** (400, 500) | Google Fonts, OFL |
| Body and UI | **Inter** (400, 500, 600) | Google Fonts, OFL |
| Labels and numbers | **JetBrains Mono** (400, 500) | Google Fonts, OFL |

The serif carries the voice; it's what a well-edited annual report feels like. Inter keeps everything else plain. The mono face is the engineer's signature: eyebrows, captions, figures and formulas, set in capitals with wide tracking.

| Element | Desktop | Mobile | Line height |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hero headline (Newsreader 400) | 132 px | 52 px | 1.0 |
| Statement (Newsreader 400) | 64 px | 34 px | 1.12 |

| Eyebrow (JetBrains Mono 500, caps, +0.18em) | 13 px | 12 px | 1.4 |

Use tabular figures for any column of numbers.

## 5. Imagery and illustration

- **Card wall.** Flat colour cards with one simple geometric shape (triangle, pentagon, diamond, half-circle). It's the brand's signature pattern: use it for LinkedIn banners, carousel covers and slide dividers.
- **Charts as illustration.** Bars, waterfalls and trend lines drawn flat in brand colours, with ink outlines. No 3D, no gradients.
- **The painted hero.** A hand-painted animation (bar-chart seedlings growing) for the website. It's the one playful piece; keep it to the website.
- **Photography.** A real headshot in natural light, plain warm background, shoulders up. Desk shots should show real work (printouts, a laptop with a model open), not staged handshakes.
- **Icons.** Outline, 2 px stroke, round caps, 24 px grid.

## 6. Voice and tone

Our voice is **plain, exact and kind**.

| We are | We are not |
|---|---|
| Specific ("cut close time from 9 days to 6") | Vague ("drove efficiencies") |
| Confident | Boastful |
| Plain English | Jargon for its own sake |
| Generous with method | Gatekeeping |

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Calm, direct | "Budgets that hold up when the year doesn't." |
| LinkedIn cheat-code | Teacherly, quick | "Colour your inputs blue. Hardcodes hide in plain sight." |
| Board slide | Headline first | "Revenue is 4% ahead of budget; marketing spend explains half." |
| Email | Warm, short | "Happy to walk you through the model on Thursday." |

**Rules:** active voice; sentences under 20 words; numbers over adjectives; no em-dash chains; no "leverage, seamless, unlock, elevate". Run `npm run slop` on website copy before publishing.

## 8. Do's and don'ts

**Do**
- Keep pages mostly ivory and ink; let colour come in blocks.
- Give each chart colour one meaning and keep it.
- Use one gold accent per view.
- Put a number in every claim you can back up.

**Don't**
- Use more than one card-wall moment per page or slide.
- Set text in gold on a light background, or put text on the mint or sky shapes.
- Mix in other fonts, gradients or drop shadows (the hard ink shadow on cards and buttons is the one exception).
- Publish an unverified number. If you can't evidence it, cut it.
